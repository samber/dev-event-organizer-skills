# The post-event sponsor report

The data-backed post-event report inside 72 hours is the highest-leverage renewal action available to an organizer - "sell the logo and you get a one-year sponsor; sell the data and you get a partner". High-performing events aim for ≥70% sponsor renewal per edition. Sponsors renew on demonstrated ROI, and they decide on a budget cycle - a report that waits for next year's planning anchors nothing.

## Template

One document per sponsor, at the depth the report menu picked. GolfStatus's and Sponsorship Collective's own published sponsor report templates use this same summary-perks-audience-outcomes-photos-next-edition shape, which confirms it as a real, adopted structure rather than an untested one:

1. **Thanks and one-line summary** - what their sponsorship made possible, concretely ("your workshop filled the largest room we had").
2. **Delivered vs promised** - their matrix column as a table: perk, delivered date, evidence (photo, link, screenshot), and what it was worth against what they paid. A slipped perk appears here too, named by you, with its make-good - before the sponsor discovers it. Sponsorship Collective's template prices each delivered perk against what the sponsor was charged, which sharpens the ROI argument past a plain delivered/not-delivered mark - carry that value column here when the extra row work is worth it to the sponsor relationship.
3. **The audience, in aggregate** - attendance, role mix, seniority mix, company-size mix. Aggregate demographics are the honest substitute for lead data at a community event; never a name, never a list.
4. **Their outcomes, against their own metric, labeled for what it can and can't prove** - scored against what this sponsor said success looks like on the exploratory call: raffle entries handed off, workshop attendance, pitch-slot audience size, booth conversations their staff logged, job-post views. Report the real number even when small - a smaller, cleaner number beats a bigger invented one, and the sponsor's own team will sanity-check whatever you send. State plainly what each number shows and does not show ("workshop attendance shows session draw, not lead quality") - HeySummit's report template makes this interpretation explicit, and it is what stops a sponsor from over-reading a raw count as more than it is.
5. **Photos** - their booth, their speaker, their raffle winner, their logo in place. Evidence collected at delivery time makes this section free.
6. **Next edition, lightly** - dates and the note that early commitments get first pick of capped surfaces. The ask itself, its timing against the sponsor's budget cycle, and the negotiation are `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-sponsor-outreach`'s motion, not this document's job.

Standard metrics like product usage, adoption, and health scores do not apply to sponsorships - a sponsorship has no usage telemetry. Substitute the sponsor's stated metric; where none was captured, say so in the report rather than inventing a proxy.

Event platforms built for larger, funnel-driven sponsorships (booth leads, meeting attribution) increasingly replace the document entirely with a live dashboard instead of a delivered report - a fit for events selling pipeline access, not for the perk-delivery-and-relationship report this template serves.

## The renew / renegotiate / drop read

The sponsor's own post-event decision has three outcomes - renew (their threshold met), renegotiate (right audience, wrong package), or drop (audience mismatch). Write your read of each sponsor's likely position when handing the report to `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-sponsor-outreach`:

- A sponsor trending **renew** gets the report and next edition's dates - don't oversell.
- A sponsor trending **renegotiate** is savable in the report itself: propose the swap before they ask - a lower tier plus the add-on that actually produced their outcomes, a workshop instead of a booth. Losing a tier beats losing the sponsor.
- A sponsor trending **drop** on audience mismatch is better released honestly than re-sold - a mismatched renewal churns next year with a worse story attached.

This three-way read mirrors the sponsor's own decision model - its value is anticipating the conversation outreach is about to have.

## Negative example

A bad report, and why each choice backfires:

- **Lists every perk with no evidence** - unevidenced claims read as unverified.
- **Pads the outcomes section with unmeasurable global-sounding metrics** ("an estimated 500,000 brand impressions") - invented reach numbers collapse the credibility of the real numbers beside them.
- **Closes with a hard renewal pitch** - turns a value document into a sales document; the sponsor's guard goes up exactly where their goodwill was highest.
