# Pipeline, tracking, and the renewal motion

## Scoring the candidate list

Source 2-3 times more candidates than the sponsors needed. Qualification culls hard. Score each:

- **Hot** - strong fit (the company's product or hiring demonstrably needs this audience) + a visible buying signal (sponsored an adjacent event, hiring for roles this audience holds, a funding round, a product launch aimed at this stack) + a reachable decision maker or warm path.
- **Warm** - fit, with a softer or older signal.
- **Cold** - loose fit or no signal.
- **Skip** - a disqualifier: no audience overlap, a conduct history incompatible with the event's code of conduct, or a segment already over-concentrated on the list.

**Evidence discipline:**

- Every qualification claim gets a source URL and date with a high/medium/low confidence mark.
- Contacts from public business channels only: no scraping, no purchased lists.
- Never infer or target on protected or sensitive traits.

**Lead-sheet output:** the scored list plus a "top outreach targets" callout naming the 3-5 hottest leads and one line each on why they go first. Diversify the sheet across startups, midsized companies, and large enterprises - a list concentrated in one segment concentrates the event's revenue risk in that segment's next budget cycle.

## Backward pipeline math

Size the list from the goal, not the other way around:

```
sponsorship revenue goal ÷ average package price = sponsors needed
sponsors needed ÷ assumed close rate            = prospects to pitch
prospects to pitch ÷ assumed reply rate          = target-list size
```

**The caveat: both rates are self-set assumptions.** Event sponsorship publishes no close-rate or reply-rate benchmark of its own, and the figures usually attached to this formula (9-15% reply, 20-30% close) are B2B SaaS sales benchmarks. Pick conservative assumptions, write them down as assumptions, and replace them with your own observed edition-over-edition rates - the only sponsorship-specific benchmarks you will ever have.

Worked example (invented, illustrative only):

1. €40,000 goal ÷ €4,000 average package = 10 sponsors.
2. At an assumed 25% close: 40 prospects to pitch.
3. At an assumed 33% first-touch-to-call rate: a list of ~120 candidates.
4. The 2-3x sourcing rule means gathering 250-350 names to qualify down from that list.

## Tracking

- Track every prospect and its stage in a CRM, an email follow-up tool, or a manual spreadsheet: the tool matters less than the habit. Pipeline tracking is a named, required step, not an afterthought.
- Name **one dedicated liaison per sponsor relationship** - a specific organizer who owns contact, payment chasing, and discount codes for that sponsor. This is an ownership practice, distinct from the tool choice.
- Suggested stage set, mirroring the workflow (with reason and objection tag at the lost stage):
  1. Identified.
  2. Qualified/scored.
  3. First touch.
  4. Call held.
  5. Proposal sent.
  6. Committed.
  7. Signed.
  8. Lost.

## Close mechanics on a yes

The moment a sponsor says yes:

1. **Invoice immediately.**
2. Generate a written confirmation of amount and payment method.
3. Verify the receiving account and currency actually work for the organizing entity, _before_ payment is sent.

Then hand the relationship to the agreement process for terms, and keep the liaison assignment through fulfillment.

## The renewal motion

**Boundary:** fulfillment _produces_ the renewal ask, the post-event report and the evidence that makes a returning sponsor say yes again. Outreach _executes_ the contact motion for renewal leads exactly as it does for net-new ones. The two skills meet at the report: renewal outreach without it is just a cold email to someone who happens to know you.

**The shortened sequence:** a renewal lead skips research (the target is known) and skips the cold first touch - the community's core-organizer network is a direct path to a company that sponsored before. Entry point is at or near the exploratory call: a conversation anchored on last edition's report, this edition's changes, and the same budget-cycle question as net-new.

**Timing:** renewal and net-new run on different clocks, not the same T-8 checkpoint. Renewal outreach starts at or within days of event close, anchored to the post-event report while the sponsor's ROI evidence is still fresh - waiting three months and skipping that report is the named failure mode (Sponsorship Collective's "Sponsorship Renewal in 90 Days"). Net-new prospecting keeps its own lead time indexed to the sponsor's fiscal-year type: 6-9 months for an anchor-tier sponsor, 3-4 months for a supplementary one, with six weeks out named as almost always too late; a calendar-year sponsor budget favors outreach in Q3 or early Q4 of the prior year, a financial-year budget favors Q1 (WW Sponsorship). Fold T-8 into the net-new track only.

**Pipeline hygiene:** track renewal and net-new as separate motions with separate stage-conversion rates. Renewal conversion is also a demand signal about the event itself, not just about outreach quality - that reading belongs to `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-market-fit`.
